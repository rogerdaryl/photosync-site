PhotoSync

Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 2, 2026

PhotoSync is a Windows application that runs entirely on your own computer. This policy explains exactly what data it touches, where it goes, and what it doesn't do.

What PhotoSync does

PhotoSync watches folders you choose on your PC and uploads new or changed photos and videos to your own Google Photos account, using the official Google Photos Library API. You sign in with your own Google account and grant PhotoSync permission directly through Google's own sign-in screen.

What permissions PhotoSync requests from Google

  • photoslibrary.appendonly — lets PhotoSync add new photos/videos to your library. This is an upload-only permission: PhotoSync cannot read, browse, or download your existing Google Photos library through this permission.
  • userinfo.email — lets PhotoSync show which Google account is currently signed in.

Where your data lives

Everything PhotoSync stores lives locally on your own PC:

  • A local database tracking which files have already been uploaded (so the same photo isn't uploaded twice), stored at %LOCALAPPDATA%\PhotoSync\photosync.db.
  • Your Google sign-in token, encrypted using Windows' built-in Data Protection API (DPAPI), tied to your Windows user account. This token is never transmitted anywhere except directly to Google's own servers when PhotoSync needs to refresh your sign-in or upload a file.
  • Local log files at %LOCALAPPDATA%\PhotoSync\logs\, used only for troubleshooting on your own machine.

What PhotoSync does not do

  • No analytics, telemetry, crash reporting, or usage tracking of any kind.
  • No server operated by the developer — PhotoSync talks directly to Google's API from your PC. The developer has no access to your photos, files, or Google account.
  • No sharing or selling of data to any third party, because no data is ever collected by the developer in the first place.

Uninstalling

Removing PhotoSync and deleting the folders above removes all local data it stored. Revoking PhotoSync's access from your Google Account permissions page immediately invalidates its ability to upload on your behalf.
